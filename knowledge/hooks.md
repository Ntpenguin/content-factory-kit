# Hooks: types, formulas and how to pick

Source ids in brackets point to `sources.md`. Everything from a video is that speaker's claim.

## 1. Match the hook to what the viewer already knows [S1]

The Goated Ads walk-through pairs five awareness levels with five hook types:

| Viewer is | Knows | Hook type | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Nothing about the problem | **Curiosity** | An odd fact, scene or question from their day |
| Problem-aware | The pain, not a fix | **Pain** | Name the moment it hurts |
| Solution-aware | Fixes exist | **Promise** | What changes, stated plainly |
| Product-aware | You exist | **Proof** | Show it working |
| Most aware | Wants it | **Offer** | The deal and the next step |

- Offer hooks go to warm audiences only. Cold audiences get curiosity and pain.
- For a service with no testimonials and no price allowed in ads (Built with Baron today), "proof" means a labelled demo and "offer" means the free survey or the test line.

## 2. The matrix [S1]

- 50 hooks x 3 to 5 bodies ("meats") x 1 to 3 calls to action = 150 to 750 ads from one batch of writing.
- The hook is tested first; a winning hook is then tried on other bodies.
- File names carry the parts: `H11-M1-A`.
- The speaker gives no budget or result numbers. Size the matrix to what can be tested (see `platforms.md`: 3 to 6 concepts per ad set on a small budget).

## 3. Three hooks by what they recognise [S7, secondhand]

- **Problem** hooks name a pain. **Identity** hooks call out who the viewer is. **Belief** hooks say out loud what the viewer believes now, so the body can change it.
- The summary says that video's winners all opened on a belief. Unverified: no link on file.
- Belief brief: the belief walking in, a reframe delivered as proof or surprise, the thought they leave with.

## 4. Ten hook types seen across many ads [S2]

Dara Denney's list (consumer e-commerce; her counts are not verified):

1. **Investment**: "I spent X on / tried N of these so you don't have to."
2. **Scam**: "I thought this was a scam until…"
3. **Visual**: something odd on screen before any word. Her claim: the visual beats the words.
4. **Give me X time**: "Give me 30 seconds and I'll show you…"
5. **POV, you hate X**: the viewer's annoyance acted out.
6. **Founder**: the founder on camera, with name and "founder" in the on-screen text.
7. **In action**: the product already working when the video starts.
8. **Emotional**: a feeling first, the product second.
9. **Why did no one tell me**: discovery framing.
10. **If you…**: a condition that picks the viewer ("If you run a plumbing truck…").

The first three seconds are four things at once: text on screen, sound, the visual, and the vibe [S2].

**Not usable as written for a brand with no customers:** "investment" and "scam" need a real person's real experience. An AI person may not say them (`compliance.md`). The founder hook is usable: Nate is real.

## 5. Villain versus hero [S3]

Order: hook (visual and words planned separately), villain, hero, demo or proof, call to action; an optional line that answers one objection.

- The villain is a habit or process (voicemail, phone tag), never a named competitor.
- The villain appears before the product is named.
- Every claim gets its proof; numbers come from `facts.md`.
- Read hook rate together with hold rate: a hook that stops people who then leave is not a win. No thresholds were given.

## 7. Formulas (fill the brackets from the brand's ICP and facts)

Curiosity
- "[Time]. [Place the owner is]. The phone rings." (a scene, no claim)
- "This is what your [trade] line sounds like at [time]." (then the labelled demo)
- "Watch what happens to a call nobody picks up."

Pain
- "[Trade word]. Hands full. Phone ringing."
- "Voicemail." as the only caption over the missed call.
- "They didn't leave a message. They called the next shop." (needs the fact that says so)

Promise (only what a fact supports)
- "Your phone still rings first. This only takes the calls you can't."
- "A setting on your line. Off any time."

Proof
- "Demo call · AI voice" over the real demo audio.
- "Send us your website. Hear a line answer as your shop."

Offer
- "Free. About 60 seconds. Six taps."

Contrast and identity [S8, generalised from real viral lines]
- "[Task] the old way vs. now."
- "Every [trade] needs to hear this."
- "POV: you're [on a roof] and the phone rings."

Other angles on file [S5, one tool's claims]: a named-rival callout (not allowed for these brands), a comparison to a physical object, reframing the product as a tool for another group, and leaving one visible flaw so the piece does not look over-polished.

## 8. What failed on film [S6]

- Opening with "AI that sounds just like a human, nobody can tell" got flat or hostile answers on about 14 filmed cold calls. Lead with the missed call, not the AI.
- The strongest line in that video: "If I needed service right now, I'd call a competitor."
- A yes-or-no question about calls ("Are you taking all the calls right now?") got the only callback.

## 9. Craft rules for any hook

- First frame is the payoff or the problem, never a logo [S8, S9].
- Text on screen in the first second, under 8 words [S9, Nate's plan].
- It must work with the sound off [S9].
- Name the trade in the first three seconds when the ad is for one trade [brand ICP; a guess].
- Every hook is logged with its type, awareness level, evidence and target customer, so results can be scored against it later.

## 10. Four Meta ad hooks one creator says tested best [S15]

From a transcript Nate pasted on 2026-11-17 ("you can use them for your next round of meta ads"). The speaker says these four won out of all the hooks they tested; no numbers, budget or industry are given, and the examples are home services (a bathroom remodel).

| Formula | Shape | The speaker's reason | Closest type in section 1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Transformation** | "I went from [before] to [after] in [timeframe] without [common misconception]." Not "we remodel bathrooms" but "I turned this old tub into a walk-in shower in two days." | Specific beats general | Proof |
| **Promise** | "We can [do service] in [timeframe] so you can [desired result]." | Says what you do, how fast, and why it matters to them | Promise |
| **Negative** | "I hated working with [type of company] until I learned this." | People want to know what to watch out for before buying | Curiosity or pain |
| **Visual** | "Does your [thing or place] look like this, or this? Want it to look like this? Here's how we fix it in under [time]." | The viewer sees the problem before understanding the service | Curiosity or pain |

How they fit these brands (the factory's rules, not the speaker's):
- Every timeframe and result must be in `facts.md`. Built with Baron today has "within one business day" (the test line, the survey reply), "about 60 seconds" (the survey) and "within a minute" (the text back); it has no customer result, so a transformation hook can only show what was built (the Fast Fix Work site) or the missed call's two endings, labelled as an illustration.
- The negative hook is a first-person story. Only Nate can say it on camera, and only if it is true; an AI voice or presenter may not claim an experience. The villain stays a habit (voicemail, phone tag, an answering service that reads a script), never a named rival (section 5).
- The visual hook needs two pictures the viewer can read with the sound off: for a phone service, a missed-call screen beside a booked-job screen; for Fast Fix Work, a real before and after from a job on file.
- Log the formula in the hook's `note` ("Formula: negative, S15") so results can be scored against it.
- The same transcript ends with a pitch for an AI video tool ("comment … and I'll send you the tool") that remakes someone else's working ad with your product. Not evaluated, not installed, and copying a rival's ad is not how this factory works.

## 13. The script format for every organic piece: HOTTI [S19]

Nate, 2026-11-20 about 9:00 AM CT: "here is the format of all the organic content should go through to make scripts", and of the scripts: "it should open loops to keep the viewer engaged". Every organic script (video, and a carousel as far as it applies) is written in this order:

| Beat | What the reel says | In this factory |
|---|---|---|
| **H: Hook** | Four at once: a visual hook, a spoken hook, an on-screen text hook and a caption hook. "None of those hooks should be the same." | Visual: the realistic clip. On-screen text: the sourced figure, counted up, source on screen (section 11). Spoken: a different line, from section 12 or section 10, not the figure read aloud. Caption: a fourth angle in the caption's first line. Four wordings, one subject |
| **O: Open loop** | One sentence straight after the hook that makes people watch to the end: "they can't stop watching until they've closed it" | One spoken sentence that promises something the video then gives, at the end of the takeaway ("The third fix is free, and it is the one to do today"). The video must close it before the invitation. Never promise more than the lesson holds ("something crazy" is out) |
| **T: Trust tag** | Who you are, what you do, why people should trust you, right after the open loop | One line from `facts.md` only (F01, F02, F17; F23 as proof of work). Default, in the brand's voice because the voice is AI: "This is Built with Baron, Nate Baron's Austin studio. We set up AI receptionists for home-service shops." No customer counts, no results, no "trusted by" (N06, N08) |
| **T: Takeaway** | "The meat of the post. What are you giving them?" | The board lesson: two or three steps, each with its source in the batch's `evidence.md`. The step that closes the loop comes last |
| **I: Invitation** | "Follow for more [topic]" | "Follow for more [topic]." then call-out D from `brand.json`: "Want a hand? Check out Built with Baron." (his ask of 2026-11-19). No "Comment WORD" until he answers that hub item |
